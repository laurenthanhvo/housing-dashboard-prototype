Data & Methods

How this housing dashboard prototype is organized

This page documents the first-version data stack, expected refresh burden, and caveats that should appear in the public dashboard. The goal is not to make a perfect real-time tracker, but to give users a clear, maintainable, and well-caveated view of housing supply in San Diego.

Version 1 data stack

RHNA / APR
Primary source for regional housing targets, progress, income tiers, and annual production signals. Best for countywide and jurisdiction-level comparisons.
City permits
Used for project/pipeline and ADU-style drilldowns where fields are available. Requires recurring QA because statuses and categories can change.
ACS / DOF
Provides population, household, housing-stock, rent, and other context indicators so users do not read supply counts in isolation.
Boundaries
Municipal, place, and county layers support mapping, joins, and click-to-filter behavior.

Deferred modules

Expiring LIHTC, public affordable housing inventory, NOAH estimates, and cross-city local portals are important but should be added after the core dashboard is stable. These sources often need special licensing, privacy review, manual reconciliation, or more frequent QA.

Required caveats

  • APR/RHNA data is self-reported by jurisdictions and may lag the current year.
  • Permit and pipeline records can change as projects are revised, withdrawn, issued, or closed.
  • ACS context indicators are estimates and should be shown with margins of error where feasible.
  • Parcel or boundary layers do not count housing units by themselves; they need to be joined to permit or production data.
  • Sensitive preservation-risk datasets should be aggregated geographically unless redistribution and privacy rules are clear.
